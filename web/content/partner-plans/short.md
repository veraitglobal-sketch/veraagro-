# Short-term business plan

_Period: 3 years · Bio Vera_

---

## 1. Introduction

Bio Vera is an organised, controlled system for the production, inspection, and distribution of agricultural products oriented toward the European market.

The model connects growers, local partners, and buyers through:

- clearly defined quality standards,
- precise process management,
- centralised sales.

Bio Vera’s objective is not trade alone: it is to build a stable system that enables reliable production, consistent quality, and secure delivery over the long term.

This business plan covers system development over **three years**.

---

## 2. Strategic objective

Over three years Bio Vera aims to build a functional, sustainable system that can scale further.

### Primary objectives

| Priority | Description |
| -------- | ----------- |
| Network | Build a reliable supplier and grower network |
| Standards | Establish clear production and inspection standards |
| Sales | Develop stable sales channels in Europe |
| Operations | Prove operational viability of the model |
| Scaling | Create the foundation for further expansion |

### Focus of the period

The focus is **not rapid growth**, but **stability**, **control**, and **repeatability** of the system.

## 4. Business model

Bio Vera manages the full value chain through clearly separated roles.

### Bio Vera

- manages sales and marketing  
- receives and plans orders  
- defines production standards  
- organises training  
- controls quality  
- organises logistics  

### Supplier

The supplier is a local partner who:

- already has an established grower network  
- distributes seed, inputs, and packaging  
- develops the local network  
- transfers system standards  

> The supplier is the **critical link** between the system and production.

### Grower

The grower is responsible for:

- production  
- adherence to standards  
- packing  
- product quality  

Goods that do not meet the standard **do not enter the system**.

## 5. Production strategy

Bio Vera applies a focused product approach that enables stability and control.

### Core products

The backbone of the system consists of:

| Product |
| ------- |
| Potato |
| Pepper |
| Tomato |
| Onion |
| Cabbage |

These products have stable demand, mature production bases, and suit standardisation and logistics.

### Operational approach

- **Core products** carry the system and deliver volume.  
- **Secondary products** are introduced selectively.  
- **New products** are piloted before scaling.  

---

## 6. Supplier model

The supplier model is **selective** and **controlled**.

### Supplier profile

A supplier must:

- have a developed grower network  
- have a strong reputation  
- have sector experience  
- be financially stable  
- operate in an agricultural region  

### Year-one structure

| Parameter | Plan |
| --------- | ---- |
| Number of suppliers | 3 |
| Coverage | Each covers one region |
| Growers per supplier | Approximately 5 |
| Total growers | Up to **15** in the system |

### Revenue model

The supplier earns stable revenue through:

- seed distribution  
- input distribution  
- packaging distribution  
- grower network development  

Revenue accrues across multiple phases of the cycle, supporting continuity and stability.

---

## 7. Grower network development

Grower network development is planned over three years.

### Year 1

**Up to 15 growers** · roughly five growers per supplier.

**Focus:**

- system testing  
- quality validation  
- learning through practice  

### Year 2

**From 15 to 30 growers.**

**Focus:**

- expansion within existing regions  
- growth around existing suppliers  
- strengthening local networks  

### Year 3

**From 30 to 50 growers.**

**Focus:**

- system stability  
- selection of top growers  
- efficiency gains  

### Key principle

> Growers develop **through the supplier**, not around them.

---

## 8. Operational process

The path from order to delivery follows clearly defined steps:

1. Buyer request intake  
2. Production planning  
3. Production organisation  
4. Packing  
5. Quality control  
6. Transport organisation  
7. Delivery  
8. Records and analysis  

All steps are standardised and controlled.

---

## 9. Quality and control model

Quality is built across the entire process.

### Core principle

> Only goods that meet the standard enter the system.

### Standardisation

- controlled seed  
- defined inputs  
- clear production rules  

### Inspection

Inspection occurs **before goods enter transport**.

### Rating

Growers and suppliers are assessed on:

- quality  
- reliability  
- rule adherence
